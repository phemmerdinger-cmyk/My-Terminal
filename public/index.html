ABOUT THIS PROJECT

Market Desk

Built by Peter Hemmerdinger · Finance, Bryant University

I built Market Desk to bring the information I use to follow the markets into a single dashboard, and to apply the concepts from my finance coursework to real, live data. My goal was to understand not just how a portfolio performs, but how much risk it takes to get there.

Company fundamentals & DCFSix years of statements and ratios straight from SEC 10-K filings, and a five-year DCF with bull/base/bear cases, CAPM-based WACC and a sensitivity table.
Quant backtest labMomentum, mean-reversion, moving-average, RSI, multi-factor and risk-parity strategies tested with trading costs, no look-ahead, out-of-sample and walk-forward validation.
Risk & stress testingValue at Risk, expected shortfall, correlations, concentration, and what 2008, 2020 and 2022 — or a hypothetical shock — would do to a portfolio.
Macro dashboardFed funds, inflation, jobs, GDP, the yield curve and recession signals from FRED, the Treasury and the BLS.
AI vs. S&P 500 experimentA $10,000 paper account traded all day by rules Claude designed (news, earnings, momentum, analyst ratings), measured against the index with full trade analytics.
Live marketsStreaming prices, a ranked news front page, an S&P 500 heatmap and market analysis.
Methods & technology

Valuation: unlevered free cash flow = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − CapEx − ΔNWC, discounted at WACC (CAPM cost of equity: risk-free + β × equity risk premium), with a Gordon-growth terminal value. Performance: CAGR, annualized volatility, Sharpe, Jensen's alpha and beta vs. SPY, maximum drawdown. Risk: historical VaR and expected shortfall, correlation matrices, beta-scaled and historical stress scenarios. Every formula is checked by automated tests.

Built with Node.js and plain JavaScript (no frameworks), free public data (SEC EDGAR, FRED, U.S. Treasury, BLS, Yahoo Finance, Finnhub), hosted on Render's free plan, with AI coding assistance. It runs at $0 a month: AI features that would cost money are switched off.

    Daily, monthly, quarterly — why the dates differ

    Daily series (Treasury yields, the yield curve) come from markets and update every business day. Monthly series (inflation, unemployment, consumer sentiment, the Fed funds average) are surveys or averages released a few weeks after the month ends. Quarterly GDP covers three months and is first published about a month after the quarter ends, then revised twice. So on any given day the cards describe different points in time — always check the “as of” date. Gray bands on the charts mark recessions as dated by the National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER).

    SIGNAL MATRIX // v1 · live technical scan

    Market Signals

    Every stock you follow, scored from price action: trend, momentum, volume and range — then mapped on the radar.

    HOW TO READ THISnew here? start with this · 1-minute read

    1What this tab does

    It looks at the last year of prices for every stock you follow (watchlist, portfolios and big market names) and asks: is the price trend healthy or weak right now? It doesn't read news or guess the future — it only reads the chart, the way a technical trader would.

    2The signal score

    Each stock gets one number from −100 to +100:

    • +50 to +100 Strong bullish — trending up on most measures
    • +20 to +49 Bullish — more good signs than bad
    • −19 to +19 Neutral — mixed, no clear direction
    • −20 to −49 Bearish — more weak signs than good
    • −50 to −100 Strong bearish — trending down on most measures

    3What goes into the score

    • Trend — is the price above its 50-day and 200-day average? (above = healthy)
    • Momentum (MACD) — is the recent push up or down getting stronger?
    • Last month — how much it moved in the past ~21 trading days
    • RSI — over 70 = ran up fast (could cool off); under 30 = sold off hard (could bounce)
    • 52-week range — near its yearly high scores higher than near its low
    • Volume — unusually heavy trading makes that day's move count more

    4The radar

    Each dot is a stock. Outer ring = most bullish, center = most bearish, the dashed middle ring is neutral. Dots are grouped by sector around the circle. A gold ring means you own it. Click any dot to open its details.

    5The signal feed — what the alerts mean

    • ✦ Golden cross — 50-day average crossed above the 200-day: often seen as a long-term turn up
    • ☠ Death cross — 50-day fell below the 200-day: often seen as a long-term turn down
    • ⇧ Breakout / ▲ 52-week high — price pushed above its recent highs
    • ⇩ Breakdown / ▼ 52-week low — price fell below its recent lows
    • ↗ / ↘ MACD turn — short-term momentum just flipped up or down
    • ◎ Oversold (RSI under 30) · ⚠ Overbought (RSI over 70)
    • ⚡ Unusual volume — at least 2× a normal day's trading
    • ↑ / ↓ 50-day cross — price moved above or below its 50-day average

    6How to use it

    • Check My holdings (filter above the table) for anything strongly bearish — worth a closer look at the news.
    • Use Bullish + With signals to find stocks with fresh momentum.
    • Click a stock, then Ask Claude about this to get the story behind the numbers.
    • Type any ticker in Scan any symbol to score something new.

    Signals describe what the price has done, not what it will do. Treat them as a starting point for research, not buy or sell advice.
